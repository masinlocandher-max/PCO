01

Public information only works if people can find it, understand it, and believe it.

Government does not have a shortage of information. It has a delivery problem. Information arrives late, or in the wrong language, or in a form nobody can act on, or without enough context to be trusted by the person reading it.

So I treat communication as an access problem before it is a messaging problem. Before asking how something should be said, I ask who needs it, what they already believe, and what would actually make them act.

That is not a soft concern. When public information fails, people miss deadlines, skip programs they qualify for, and fill the gap with whatever explanation reached them first.

02

I learned fact‑checking on my own hometown.

I come from Masinloc, a coastal municipality in Zambales. Most of the work I care about started with noticing something at home that I did not want to lose.

I began looking closely at local history because details kept getting repeated for years without anyone checking where they came from. Not inventions, exactly — just claims that had been said often enough to sound settled. Tracing them back taught me the difference between what is documented and what is merely repeated.

That pulled me into source checking, cultural documentation, and the preservation of Tina Sambal, the language tied to my community. I prefer preservation people can actually use. A language survives when it is spoken. History matters when people know it.

“A correction never travels as far as the original.”

Which is why verification has to happen before publication, not after.

06

What I would contribute

I would not arrive asking only what needs to be posted. Verification, language, operations and public trust affect each other, and I am used to looking at them together.

01

Verification before publication.

I would rather hold a post for an hour than spend a week recovering from it. Sourcing checks can be light enough to survive a real deadline and still catch the thing that would have cost credibility.

02

Reach past the language line.

National messaging often stops at English and Tagalog. In Zambales I have watched information die at exactly that point. Having worked on language preservation, I treat regional-language reach as a design requirement, not a translation afterthought.

03

Plain language for complicated programs.

Most public programs are not rejected. They are misunderstood and then abandoned. Rewriting for comprehension is unglamorous, measurable work, and I am willing to do it.

04

Communication that reflects operations.

An unclear advisory becomes an operational problem at the frontline, and a weak explanation becomes a trust problem afterwards. I am used to watching both at once.

05

Capacity, not dependency.

I have trained adults with very different levels of confidence. I can help agency and local staff produce their own material properly, instead of leaving them waiting on the centre.

06

A view from the ground.

I come from a municipality, not a newsroom. I know the difference between information that reached people and information that only looks like it did.

07

Being outside Metro Manila is the point.

National communication is written and judged in the capital, but it lands somewhere else. The distance between those two places is where most public messaging quietly fails.

Working from Zambales gives that work a real contact point on the ground — someone who can check whether a message arrived, in what form, and what people understood it to mean.

Regional and local government communication coordination

Community-level checks on what actually reached people

Regional-language and cultural context

Local partner, supplier and creative coordination

Field documentation, photo and video capture

Rapid regional response during urgent advisories

The value is not only proximity. I understand communication, public perception, culture and verification, so I can represent the office’s standards properly while working outside it.

08

I am not applying to make government look good.

I am applying to make government information usable.

I would bring research discipline, public-facing experience, teaching, creative direction, and a perspective from outside the capital. I would also bring the habit of asking one question whenever I join a project: how do we make this clearer for the person who has to rely on it?
