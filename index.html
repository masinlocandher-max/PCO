I am applying to work with the Miss Intercontinental organization — in communications, creative direction and regional support. Not as a delegate. This page explains who I am, what I have done, and what I could contribute.

What I could contribute ↘

01

Masinloc made me pay attention.

I come from Masinloc, a coastal municipality in Zambales. Most of the work I care about started with noticing something at home that I did not want to lose.

I began looking closely at local history because details kept getting repeated for years without anyone checking where they came from. That pulled me into source checking, cultural documentation, and the preservation of Tina Sambal, the language tied to my community.

I prefer preservation that people can actually use. A language survives when it is spoken. History matters when people know it. So I have worked on learning materials, stories and digital resources that put local knowledge within reach.

02

Community work is where my ideas get tested.

Service is not one activity to me. Sometimes it is direct outreach. Sometimes it is teaching. Sometimes it is helping someone get ready for work. Sometimes it is doing the research because people deserve a better answer than the one they have.

“How can what I know become useful to someone other than me?”

That question decides most of what I take on.

03

Education gave the work its structure.

01

Bachelor of Science in Tourism Management

Tourism taught me to think about people, destinations, hospitality, events, culture, and the way a place presents itself to the world.

02

Graduate studies in History, University of the Philippines Baguio

History sharpened how I research, compare sources, and separate what is documented from what is simply repeated.

03

Former college instructor

Teaching forced me to explain complicated things plainly, and to read a room while I was doing it.

04

Former BPO trainer, and training work with local government

Training adults with very different levels of confidence taught me pacing, patience, and how to tell when instructions are not landing.

Tourism taught me how places are presented. History taught me to ask whether the story being presented is true.

04

I have stood on that stage myself.

I competed and placed as Ms Gay Zambales 1st Runner-Up. I know what the preparation actually costs — the rehearsals, the call times, the wardrobe, the interviews, the waiting, and the part where you walk out composed anyway.

That changed how I look at delegates. Behind a polished appearance there is a person managing schedules, sponsors, supporters, media, exhaustion and expectation at the same time.

It is why I care about clear delegate communication and coordination that treats people decently. A pageant can look excellent from the audience and still be hard to be inside of. Both sides should work.

05

I can be in front of the room. I can also do the work behind it.

I have presented on stage and I have built the thing being presented. I can shape a message, direct a visual, brief a team, organise information, and still keep track of how the whole thing is landing with the people watching.

07

What I could contribute

I would not arrive asking only what needs to be posted. Communication, delegate experience, public image, sponsors and operations all affect each other, and I am used to looking at them together.

01

I connect communication and operations.

An unclear delegate brief becomes an operational problem. A weak campaign becomes a perception problem. I am used to watching both at once.

02

I understand candidates as people.

I have been on that stage. Clear schedules, useful reminders and respectful handling change how a delegate experiences an entire competition.

03

I can make international stories legible.

Delegates should not feel interchangeable. Each woman brings a country, a culture and a reason people might remember her. That is a writing and direction problem, and I can do that work.

04

I think about what a sponsor actually receives.

A sponsor should get more than a logo on a poster. I can help think through visibility, content, partnerships, and how value gets reported back to the people funding it.

05

I notice what can be improved.

I respect systems that work. I also ask questions when something is unclear or easy to strengthen. I would want to contribute to the organisation, not just complete a checklist.

08

I can cover what is simply too far from Europe.

Miss Intercontinental works internationally, but the core team cannot be everywhere. Being based in the Philippines gives the organisation a real point of contact on this side of the world.

I can take on the things that are slower, more expensive, or harder to judge from a distance — particularly where local context decides the outcome.

Philippines and Asia-based delegate coordination

Local partner, supplier and creative coordination

Media, appearance and interview support

Country-specific research and cultural context

Local sponsor and partnership follow-up

Time-zone handoff for urgent regional matters

The value is not only that I am closer. I understand communication, public perception, culture and pageantry, so I can represent the organisation's interests properly while working locally.

09

I am not looking for another pageant stage.

I am applying for the work behind it.

I would bring my experience, my understanding of pageantry, my work with people, my eye for communication, and my perspective from the Philippines. I would also bring the habit of asking one question whenever I join a project: how do we make this better?
