# FMB — Content Engine

## The required shape of every output

No exceptions, including for a one-line idea. A draft without these is
decoration, and the schema refuses to store one.

- **Objective** — what this is for. Not "engagement".
- **Audience** — who specifically, from the priority list.
- **Strategic angle** — why it is hers to say, and why now.
- **Draft** — the actual text, ready to read.
- **Recommended action** — post it, hold it, rework it, or drop it, and why.

## Post shapes that suit her

- **The correction.** A thing widely believed in her field that is wrong, and
  what is true instead. Strong because it takes a position.
- **The working note.** Something she learned doing the work this month.
  Specific, dated, small. Credibility comes from the specificity.
- **The refusal.** Something she decided not to do, and the reasoning. Rare and
  memorable, because almost nobody publishes their no.
- **The credit.** Someone else's work, named and explained. Costs nothing,
  builds everything.
- **The book line.** One idea from *The Right Way to Live*, given room. Never a
  sales post wearing a quote as a hat.

## Shapes to avoid

The engagement-bait question. The humble-brag disguised as a lesson. The listicle
with no author in it. The "I was rejected 47 times" arc. The post that could have
been written by anyone about anything.

## Carousels

Only when the idea genuinely has sequence — a process, a before and after, a
progression. A carousel of restated sentences is a post with extra steps. Cover
slide earns the swipe; last slide says what to do next.

## Visuals

Her own photography and design where possible; the site already has a strong
visual system (warm cream, gold, editorial serif). Stock imagery reads as
borrowed credibility. Text-on-image only when the text is short enough to read
at thumbnail size.

## Performance

Numbers are entered by hand from LinkedIn, or imported. Nothing is inferred. A
metric with no source is not a metric, and the analytics engine will say so
rather than fill the gap.
