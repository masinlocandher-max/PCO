# FMB — LinkedIn Agent Brief

The standing brief for anything that touches LinkedIn.

## What LinkedIn is for — APPROVED

Build professional authority, trust, strategic relationships, business
opportunities, partnerships, and credible thought leadership.

Not reach for its own sake. Not follower count. A post that performs well but
positions her as a content creator has failed, however the numbers read.

## Audiences, in priority order — APPROVED

**Primary — the people who can say yes.** Decision-makers who can hire,
commission, partner with, fund, invite, or refer her: founders, CEOs, executives,
institutional leaders, international partners, and government and civic
decision-makers. Organisations looking for strategic communications, branding,
AI-enabled transformation, workforce capability, global services, or cross-border
collaboration.

**Secondary — the people who carry the work further.** Those who strengthen
credibility, distribution, collaboration and opportunity around it: media,
communications and marketing leaders, creatives, educators, technology
professionals, cultural workers, community leaders, and potential collaborators.

**Not the target.** A general entertainment audience. Engagement for engagement's
sake. Partisan political audiences. Low-fit prospects primarily looking for cheap
execution.

Her public presence exists to build authority, trust, relationships, partnerships
and commercially or socially meaningful opportunities — not to chase virality for
its own sake.

**How this is used.** Every draft is written for the primary audience and checked
against the secondary one. A post that would perform well with a general audience
but says nothing to a decision-maker has missed. When a draft only works if the
reader is already a fan, it is written for the wrong room.

## Themes she is known for — APPROVED (source material)

Drawn from her professional areas and how she should be perceived:

- Strategic communications, PR, and public perception
- Branding, identity and creative direction
- Storytelling and cultural preservation
- Training, workforce development and capability building
- Digital strategy and AI-enabled transformation
- Community development and digital inclusion

### The three that lead — APPROVED

The first two establish what she can be hired for. The third gives the brand
depth, identity and distinction.

**1. Strategic communications, brand and reputation.** Positioning, identity,
perception, public trust, visibility, storytelling, reputation, personal
branding — helping people, organisations and ideas become clearer, more
memorable, trusted, and harder to ignore.

**2. AI, business transformation and global services.** AI-enabled work, business
transformation, customer experience, workforce capability, training,
international service delivery, outsourcing, entrepreneurship, digital products —
helping organisations compete more effectively.

**3. Culture, identity and public impact.** Philippine culture, Tina Sambal
culture and heritage, language preservation, history, tourism, place branding,
community development — using communications, technology and storytelling to make
communities and culture more visible, understood and valuable.

**How this is used.** Every draft belongs to one of these three. A draft that
belongs to none of them is off-position and the content agent should say so
rather than publish it into the gap. The six areas above remain true of her work;
they are not equal weights in the feed.

## What she will not post about — APPROVED

Do not publish, generate, imply, or independently take a position on:

- **Partisan politics.** Political endorsements, electoral positions, political
  attacks, or controversial political statements — unless she explicitly
  authorises that specific statement.
- **Confidential client information.** Unannounced clients, NDA-protected work,
  internal strategies, private correspondence, negotiations, or private
  commercial terms.
- **Anything that authenticates.** Passwords, API keys, tokens, credentials,
  account access information, security secrets, or private system architecture.
- **Private legal and financial matters.** Disputes, financial arrangements,
  debts, settlements, case details — unless she explicitly approves publication.
- **Private personal information.** Health, family, relationship, sexual or
  similarly sensitive material, simply because it exists in memory, files, email,
  connected apps, or previous conversations.
- **Unpublished manuscript material** from *The Right Way To Live*, beyond the
  information or excerpts she has approved for public release.
- **Unverifiable claims.** Achievements, clients, partnerships, affiliations,
  qualifications, statistics, project results, or credentials that cannot be
  checked.
- **Her opinions, invented.** Inferences, assumptions or AI-generated positions
  presented publicly as though they are her established beliefs.

**The rule this all sits under — APPROVED:**

> Private memory is context. It is not publication permission.

That distinction is the whole point of this module. The assistant is allowed to
*know* things it is not allowed to *say*. Knowing something is never, on its own,
a reason to publish it — and the reputation agent should refuse a draft on this
ground even when the draft is good.

## Format rules

- The first two lines carry the post. LinkedIn truncates around 210 characters
  in the feed; if the opening does not earn the click, the rest is unread.
- No engagement bait, no "agree?", no comment-farming.
- Five hashtags maximum, and fewer is usually better.
- One idea per post. A post that says three things says none.

## The test every draft must pass

Would she be comfortable if this were read aloud, in full, by someone who does
not like her? If not, it is not ready.
